How to read the October decision odds

Read the row and time

At October 1, 2026, about 19:03 UTC access, Kalshi’s October page displayed 75% “Fed maintains rate” and 25% “Hike 25bps” in named Chance headings. Polymarket’s October page displayed 75% “No change” and 25% “25 bps increase” in named rows. These are separate event-contract readings. Access time is not a quote timestamp.

Do not turn a row into a complete forecast

Kalshi lists additional outcomes and a different selected order-entry odds label; use its named Chance heading for this comparison. Polymarket’s previously inspected rules define outcomes by the target-range upper-bound change and use the October FOMC statement for resolution. Similar labels do not justify averaging venues, deriving a residual from rounded rows, or calling a price the Fed’s forecast. Polymarket’s experimental AI narrative is not evidence.

What the latest change does not prove

Since the 13:36 UTC check, Kalshi’s named hold row rose 10 percentage points and Polymarket’s rose 11. The snapshots do not show when or why. Jefferson’s October 1 speech lacks a precise time on the official page. An October decision row does not answer a year-end policy-path or asset-return question.

Keep futures-implied figures separate

CME FedWatch derives probabilities from 30-Day Fed Funds futures, not event-contract trades. Its direct October grid and quote time were unreadable here, so no live CME percentage or cross-venue spread is shown.
