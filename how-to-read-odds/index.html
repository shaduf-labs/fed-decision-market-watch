Read the rule and freshness label before the percentage

What the current 87% means here

At 13:33 UTC on September 15, the direct Kalshi September event page displayed an 87% main chance heading for “Hike 25bps” and 13% for “Fed maintains rate.” It is a separate event-contract reading. Kalshi did not display a separate quote timestamp, so the pool records the page-access time.

Why the 80% Polymarket record is not beside it as current

The last directly verified Polymarket September event page record was 80% for a 25 bp increase and 19% for no change at 19:06 UTC on September 14. The September 15 renderer returned those values but labelled its content as crawled yesterday. The pool marks the record stale instead of pretending the old value is a live quote.

The rule behind each reading

Polymarket defines its rate outcome by the upper bound of the target federal funds range and names the FOMC statement as resolution source. Kalshi’s CFTC-filed generic FEDDECISION rulebook defines FOMC target-range actions as its underlying and names the Federal Reserve System as source agency. Read the exact venue terms before treating a similar label as identical.

What they do not mean

  • They are not the Federal Reserve’s forecast.
  • They are not a futures-implied CME FedWatch estimate.
  • They do not form a consensus when averaged or displayed side by side.
  • They do not prove that a release caused the price change.
  • They are not personal investment advice.

Why time and precision matter

Prices can update after a page is checked, and a page can render an older crawl. The usable Kalshi comparison is the named 87% hike heading against its 85% direct-page record from September 14: +2 percentage points. The Polymarket 80% record is stale, and CME’s live grid was unreadable. Neither a stale record nor a blank authorizes a substitute average. Polymarket’s selected rows are rounded, so do not compute a residual outcome from them.

Why a 25 bp result is not always the next range

The July target range was 3.50%–3.75%. Under Polymarket’s upper-bound rule, a 25 bp increase would correspond to an upper-bound change from 3.75% to 4.00%. Read the venue’s full rule for rounding and fallback conditions.
