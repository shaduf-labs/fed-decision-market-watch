Three ways to read the September decision

Do not combine these into a made-up consensus. They are different instruments with different inputs, rules, and access limits.

MeasureWhat it saysMethod and limit
Polymarket: Fed Decision in September?The direct event page showed 80% for a 25 bp increase and 19% for no change when accessed Sep. 14, 2026, 13:34 UTC. Its prior direct-page record was 79% and 22% at 19:07 UTC on Sep. 13.Event-contract rows. The contract resolves on the change in the upper bound of the target range versus its pre-meeting level. The page shows no separate quote time; rounded rows are not an exact 100% total. The direct-page changes are +1 and −3 points; the separate Sep. 13 Economy listing does not enter that series.
Kalshi: Fed decision in September?Main chance headings showed 83% for “Hike 25bps” and 18% for “Fed maintains rate” when accessed Sep. 14, 2026, 13:34 UTC. The pool’s prior direct-page headings were 79% and 21%.A separate event-contract series. The page showed an 83¢ Yes quote beside the 83% hike heading, so this pool reports the headline row rather than an inferred close. Kalshi’s filed generic FEDDECISION rulebook names FOMC target-range decisions and the Federal Reserve System; the full series-specific terms were not exposed here.
CME FedWatchCurrent September figure not directly captured in this check.CME describes the tool as probabilities implied by 30-Day Fed Funds futures. Its live grid was not directly readable. No third-party rate figure is used for a spread.

What 80% and 83% do and do not say

The direct pages both show a leading named hike outcome, but their current figures differ and their contracts are separate. That does not create a pooled probability, proof of liquidity, a cross-market trade, or a forecast from the Committee. The usable changes are each venue’s direct-page comparison with its own prior recorded page access.
